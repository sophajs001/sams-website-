Theology Department

Advanced Sacred Theology Education & Priestly Formation

A comprehensive four-year program leading to Bachelor of Sacred Theology (B.ST) degree, affiliated with Pontificia Universitas Urbaniana, Rome.

Academic Programme Overview

Programme Duration & Structure

The academic programme lasts four years, divided into two semesters per year. Courses are examined at the end of each semester, with continuous assessment included throughout.

Programme Focus Areas

Major Courses
  • Scripture
  • Moral Theology
  • Dogmatic Theology
  • Liturgy
  • Church History
  • Canon Law
  • Pastoral Theology
  • Mission Theology
Supporting Areas
  • Pastoral Courses: Practical ministry preparation
  • General Studies: Research methods, languages, communication, leadership
  • Research Project: Compulsory long essay submitted in final year

Degree Award

Bachelor's Degree in Sacred Theology (B.ST) - Awarded by Pontificia Universitas Urbaniana, Rome

Degree Requirements

  • 50% average in major subjects
  • 40% in minor subjects
  • Completion of research paper
  • Pass written comprehensive exam
  • Pass oral examination

Examination Requirements

Attendance: Minimum 75% attendance required
Failing Grade: Below 40% is considered a fail
Resit Maximum: Resit grades capped at 60%
Academic Probation: Failure of multiple courses leads to repetition of the year

Mark Distribution (Final Grade)

Continuous Assessment: 30%
Written Examination: 50%
Oral Examination: 20%
TOTAL: 100%

YEAR I COURSES

Compulsory Courses (30 Credits)

REL 111 Introduction to the New Testament 3 Credits

Course Content

The Greco-Roman and Jewish worlds of the first century A.D. The origin and nature of the New Testament writings. The historico-critical methods, with particular reference to Bultmann, the new quest for the historical Jesus. Canonical criticism. The New Testament as the Word of God. Select N.T. themes, with detailed study of relevant texts.

Bibliography
  • Brown R.E., An Introduction to the New Testament (Bangalore: Theological Publications in India, 2000).
  • Brown R.E. Et al (eds.), The New Jerome Biblical Commentary (Ibadan: St. Pauls Publications, 2007).
  • Brown R.E., New Testament Documents: Are they Reliable? (Michigan: W.B. Erdman Grand Rapids, 1960).
  • Bruce F.F., New Testament History (London: Nelson Publishers, 1969).
  • Etiene Charpentier, How to Read the New Testament (Bombay: St. Paul's Publications, 1995).
  • Kimmel W., Introduction to the New Testament (London: SCM Press Ltd., 1975).
  • Luke K., The TPI Companion to the Bible, Vol. II: New Testament (Bangalore: Theological Publications in India, 1994).
REL 112 Historical Books of the Old Testament 3 Credits

Course Content

This course deals with the change from nagid (prince) to melek (king). The kingships of Saul, David and Solomon will be examined, pointing to the style of the Deuteronomic Historian. The History will continue through the fall of Samaria to the Exile.

Bibliography
  • Campbell A.F., Of Prophets and Kings (Washington: 1986).
  • Gray J., Joshua, Judges and Ruth (London: Century Books, 1967).
  • Nelson R.D., The Double Redaction of the Deuteronomic History (Sheffield: Academic Press, 1981).
  • Noth M., The Deuteronomistic History (Sheffield: Academic Press, 1981).
  • Brown R.E. et al (eds.), The New Jerome Biblical Commentary (Ibadan: St. Paul's Publications, 2007).
  • Weippert M., The Settlement of the Israelite Tribes in Palestine (London: SBT., 1980).
REL 113 Introduction to Christian Ethics 3 Credits

Course Content

This course will examine various issues in ethics, such as right and wrong in human conduct, conditions of limitations to human freedom, the place of law in Ethics, the idea of reward and punishment, and conscience as a norm of morality; it will also examine "situation ethics" with regard to absolutes in morality.

Bibliography
  • Barclay William, Ethics in a Permissive Society (London: Collin Fontana Books, 1971).
  • Dietrich and Alice Von Hildebrand, Ethics (Chicago: Franciscan Herald Press, 1966).
  • Edwin F. Healy, Moral Guidance (Illinois: Loyola University Press, 1960).
  • Eli William, Understanding Catholic Morality (New York: Cross Herder Book, 1997).
  • Haring Bernard, The Law of Christ Vol. I (Cork: The Percier Press, 1963).
  • Helmut Thielicke, Theological Ethics Vol. I (Michigan: William B. Eerdman's Publishing Company, 1966).
REL 114 Introduction to the Gospels 3 Credits

Course Content

This course examines the nature and purpose of the Four Gospels. The transmission of the primitive tradition, the relation between transmission and interpretation and the relation between the Synoptic and the Fourth Gospels. It will also examine the modern criticism of the Gospels.

Bibliography
  • Gerhardson Birger, The Origins of the Gospel Traditions (London, 1979).
  • "Instruction of the Pontifical Biblical Commission: The Interpretation of the Bible in the Church" (1994).
  • Sandy W. (ed.) Studies in the Synoptic Problem (Oxford, MCMXI).
  • Vawter Bruce, The Four Gospels: An Introduction (Dublin & Sydney, 1967).
REL 115 Ecclesiology 3 Credits

Course Content

This course shall be concerned with the etymology and the interpretations of the word "Church". The mysterious nature of the Church, and the mystery of the Church as a people gathered together by the Trinity (God). The development of Ecclesiology. Models and Paradigms of the Church. The four marks of the Church One, Holy, Catholic and Apostolic. Apostolic Succession, Papacy and Collegiality. The Laity in the Church. The Sacraments of the Church and their role in Church union and dialogue. The Holy Spirit and the Church.

Bibliography
  • Dulles Avery, Models of the Church (New York: Double Day, 1987).
  • Flannery Austin, Vatican Council II: The Conciliar and Post Conciliar Documents Vol I (Dublin: Dominican Publications, 1988).
  • McBrien Richard P. Catholicism (San Francisco: Harpercollins, 1994).
  • Paul John II, Post-Synodal Apostolic Exhortation - Ecclesia in Africa (Rome: 1995).
REL 116 Ancient & Medieval Church History 3 Credits

Course Content

The introduction to the study of Ecclesiastical history in the early and medieval periods will be the concentration of this course. Attention will be given to the period of Christ, expansion of Christianity, Christian martyrs, Christian communion, doctrinal disputes and the growth of the Church as an organization. The medieval aspect will look at the fall of the empire, the great invasions and the formation of medieval Christianity.

Bibliography
  • Gibbon Edward, The Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire (England: Penguine Books, 1985).
  • Neill Thomas P. & Schmandt Raymond H. (Eds.), History of the Catholic Church (Milwaukee: Bruce, 1957).
  • Rizzo Francesco Paolo, La Chiesa Dei Primi Secoli: Lineamenti Storici (Bari: Edipuglia, 1999).
  • Wand J.W.C., A History of the Early Church to AD 500 (New York: Methuen 1982).
REL 121 Principles of Hermeneutics 3 Credits

Course Content

The meaning of Hermeneutics and its relevance to Biblical Studies. Survey of the main trends in Biblical hermeneutics: the historico-critical method, Demythologization, recent hermeneutic trends, with particular reference to selected seminal thinkers, structuralism and the New Hermeneutics.

Bibliography
  • Benedict XV, Spiritus Paraclitus (On the Fifteenth Centenary of St. Jerome, 1920).
  • Bleicher J., Contemporary Hermeneutics: Hermeneutics as Method, Philosophy and Critique (Suffolk: Routledge and Kegan Ltd., 1980).
  • Graham H.G., Where We Got the Bible: Our Debt to the Catholic Church (London: TAN Books, 1987).
  • Mattam Z., Opening the Bible: Meeting Christ in the Scriptures (Bangalore: KJC Publications, 1998).
SEM 100 Introduction to Canon Law 3 Credits

Course Content

Topics Covered: Preliminary notions, General notions of Law, What is Canon Law and its purpose, Sources of Canon Law, The history of the 1983 Code and its principle of revision according to Vatican II, The Structure of the Code, Characteristics of Canon Law, Canonical Methodology and rules for interpretation.

Bibliography
  • Beal J. P., Coriden J. A., Green T. J., New Commentary on the Code of Canon Law (New York: Paulist Press, 2000).
  • Caparros E., Theriault M. and Thom J., eds. Canon Law Annotated (Montreal: Wilson and Lafleur Ltd., 1993).
  • Coriden James, An Introduction to Canon Law (New York: Paulist Press, 1991).
SEM 101 Liturgy 3 Credits

Course Content

Topics: General Principles of the Liturgy: Essence and purpose of the Liturgy; principles and practice of liturgical education in view of renewal using the Vatican II documents on Liturgy. The Liturgical Year: its history, theology, temporal and sanctoral reforms, effective celebration. Popular devotions in the Church.

Bibliography
  • Adolf Adam, Foundation of Liturgy: An Introduction to its History and Practice (Minnesota: The Liturgical Press, 1985).
  • Flannery Austin, ed. Vatican Council II: Conciliar and Post Conciliar Documents (New York: Costello Publishing Company, 1988).
  • Lang J. P., Dictionary of the Liturgy (NY: Catholic Book Publishing Co., 1989).
SEM 112 Fundamental Theology 3 Credits

Course Content

The definition of Fundamental Theology as a branch of Theology, its history and specific character. Special attention should be given to the theology of Revelation; the two main sources of revelation: Sacred Scripture and Sacred Tradition. The theology of faith, religious experience, Dei Verbum of Vatican II Council should be closely studied.

Bibliography
  • Congregation for the Doctrine of Faith, Dominus Iesus: On the Unicity and Salvific Universality of Jesus Christ and the Church (Vatican City, 2000).
  • Fires Heinrich, Fundamental Theology (Washington D.C.: The Catholic University of American Press, 1985).
  • Rahner K., Foundations of Christian Faith (New York: Crossroad Publishing Co., 1994).
GSS 001 The Use of English (2 semesters) 3 Credits

Course Content

Basic elements of English grammar, with particular reference to structure, types of sentences, punctuation, and common faults. Reading efficiency, and note-taking. Writing skills including the preparation of essays and examinations. Summary, Comprehension and the development of vocabulary.

GSS 004 Interpersonal Communication 3 Credits

Course Content

This course studies the role and the place of the art of communication in the social environment. Areas to be studied include the types and forms of interpersonal communication, self-esteem and communication, inter-human relationships and communication, listening skills, the communication climate, managing interpersonal conflict through communication.

Elective Courses - Within Major Subject Area (2-3 Credits)

REL 117 The Rise and Spread of Islam 2 Credits

Course Content

This course deals with events in Arabia before the advent of Islam; it also examines the life of the Prophet Muhammad, the Quran and the Pillars of Islam.

REL 122 Introduction to New Testament Greek Morphology 3 Credits

Course Content

Introduction to the Greek alphabet, declensions, conjugations, basic grammar and syntax.

Elective Courses - Outside Major Subject Area (2 Credits)

REL 118 Basic Concepts of African Religion 2 Credits

Course Content

This is an examination of correct approaches to the study of Religions in general and African Traditional Religion in particular. It will examine the definitions and origin of Religion and nature of African Religion.

REL 119 Problems of Philosophy 2 Credits

Course Content

This course will examine: The problem of evil - the position of Augustine and Schleimacher. The soul, immortality, resurrection: the problem of miracles: religion and science: mythology in religion.

YEAR II COURSES

Compulsory Courses (32 Credits)

REL 211 Old Testament Wisdom Literature 3 Credits

Course Content

A literary, historical and theological study of the books of Proverbs, Job, Ecclesiastes and Song of Songs. Their wisdom tradition and literary forms. The figure of Wisdom personified. The theology of the wisdom books as it relates to the Hellenistic world and to Jewish thought.

Bibliography
  • Clifford R.E., Wisdom Literature (Dublin: Gill and Macmillan, 1999).
  • Donne R., The Wisdom Literature (London: Theological Publications, 1998).
  • Emmet Dorothy, The Role of Aesthetics in Moral Theory (London: Macmillan Press Ltd., 1994).
REL 212 The Pauline Epistles 3 Credits

Course Content

A systematic study of the authorship, date and character of the Pauline epistles and their historical situation. Detailed study of selected epistles: themes such as justification by faith, Christian living, church authority, eschatology. The problem of the disputed epistles: Colossians, Ephesians, Thessalonians and the Pastoral Epistles.

Bibliography
  • Brown R.E., An Introduction to the New Testament (Bangalore: Theological Publications in India, 2000).
  • Fitzmeyer J.A., Romans: A New Translation with Introduction and Commentary (New York: Doubleday, 1993).
  • Kummel W.G., Introduction to the New Testament (London: SCM Press Ltd., 1975).
REL 213 Dogmatic Theology I: God and Creation 3 Credits

Course Content

The proofs for God's existence and nature. The attributes of God: immutability, infinity, simplicity and impassibility. The Trinity in the Old Testament, New Testament and in Post-Nicene Theology. The doctrine of creation, ex nihilo, as found in Biblical sources and later development in Christian Theology. Creation in time versus eternity. The problem of evil and theodicy.

Bibliography
  • Flannery A., ed. Vatican Council II: Conciliar and Post-Conciliar Documents (Dublin: Dominican Publications, 1975).
  • Rahner K., Foundations of Christian Faith (New York: Crossroad Publishing Co., 1994).
REL 214 Church History: Reformation Period 3 Credits

Course Content

The causes of the Protestant Reformation and the main Reformers: Luther, Calvin, and Zwingli. Their theological positions and their challenges to the Church. The Church's response: the Catholic Reformation and the Council of Trent. The Counter-Reformation and the activity of the Jesuits and other Counter-Reformation figures.

Bibliography
  • Latourette K.S., A History of Christianity (New York: Harper and Row Publishers, 1953).
  • Neill T.P. and Schmandt R.H., eds. History of the Catholic Church (Milwaukee: Bruce, 1957).
REL 224 Pastoral Theology 3 Credits

Course Content

The pastoral office and the pastoral role in the world. The sacramental function of the priest. The ministerial function of the priest. Pastoral care: spiritual direction, confession and counseling. Principles and practice of pastoral education. The sacrament of penance and reconciliation. The sacrament of the sick and anointing of the sick.

Bibliography
  • Flannery A., ed. Vatican Council II: Conciliar and Post-Conciliar Documents (New York: Costello Publishing Company, 1988).
SEM 200 Christology 3 Credits

Course Content

The historical Jesus in the New Testament and the Christ of faith. The incarnation as the central mystery of Christianity. The nature and purpose of the incarnation. Hypostatic union. The problem of Christ's knowledge and will. Kenosis and communicatio idiomatum. The resurrection of Christ and its theological implications. Christ as the fulfillment of Old Testament prophecy.

Bibliography
  • Brown R.E., The Birth of the Messiah (New York: Doubleday, 1993).
  • Grillmeier A., Christ in Christian Tradition Vol. I (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 1989).
  • Rahner K., Foundations of Christian Faith (New York: Crossroad Publishing Co., 1994).
SEM 201 Pneumatology and Mariology 3 Credits

Course Content

Pneumatology: The role of the Holy Spirit in Christian theology. The Holy Spirit in the Old Testament, Gospels and Epistles. The Holy Spirit and the Church. The gifts of the Holy Spirit. Sanctification and Charisma. Mariology: The place of Mary in Christian theology. The Marian doctrines: Incarnation, Virgin birth, Perpetual virginity, Immaculate Conception, Assumption. Mary as Mother of God, Queen of Heaven and Intercessor.

Bibliography
  • Boff L., The Maternal Face of God (San Francisco: Harper and Row, 1987).
  • Flannery A., ed. Vatican Council II: Conciliar and Post-Conciliar Documents (New York: Costello Publishing Company, 1988).
SEM 211 Introduction to Moral Theology 2 Credits

Course Content

Overview of Moral Theology. The natural law and its application to ethics. The Ten Commandments as the foundation of Christian morality. The Sermon on the Mount and the Beatitudes. Virtue ethics: virtues and vices. The development of conscience and moral decision-making. Human dignity and the value of the human person.

Bibliography
  • Haring B., The Law of Christ Vol. I (Cork: The Mercier Press, 1963).
  • John Paul II, Encyclical Letter Veritatis Splendor (Rome, 1993).
GSS 002 The Study of History 2 Credits

Course Content

Historical method and analysis. Sources and evidence in history. Chronology and periodization. Causation and change in history. Interpretation and historiography. The role of the historian. History and theology.

GSS 003 Academic Writing 2 Credits

Course Content

Introduction to scholarly writing. Thesis development. Organization and structure of academic essays. Research methods and source documentation. Critical reading and analysis. Proper citation methods (including footnotes and bibliography). Editing and proofreading.

GSS 005 African Socio-Cultural Studies 2 Credits

Course Content

Overview of African society and cultures. African anthropology and ethnography. Traditional structures and values. The impact of colonialism. Post-colonial development. African theology and inculturation. The role of the Church in African society.

Elective Courses - Within Major Subject Area (2-3 Credits)

REL 215 Old Testament Prophecy 3 Credits

Course Content

The nature and function of Old Testament prophecy. The origins of Hebrew prophecy. The major and minor prophets. Prophecy and history. The Messianic prophecies. Prophecy and fulfillment in Christian theology.

REL 221 Non-Canonical Writings (Apocrypha) 2 Credits

Course Content

The inter-testamental period. The Dead Sea Scrolls and other contemporary writings. The Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha. Their historical and theological significance. Their relationship to canonical scriptures.

Elective Courses - Outside Major Subject Area (2 Credits)

REL 222 The Eastern Churches 2 Credits

Course Content

The Eastern Orthodox Church: history, theology, worship and practice. The relationship between Eastern and Western Christianity. The schism of 1054. Contemporary Eastern Christianity and ecumenical dialogue. The Eastern Catholic churches.

REL 223 Buddhism and Other Eastern Religions 2 Credits

Course Content

The life of Buddha and the origins of Buddhism. Buddhist doctrines: Four Noble Truths, Eight-fold Path, Karma and Rebirth. Theravada and Mahayana Buddhism. Buddhism in Asia and the West. Other Eastern religions: Taoism, Shintoism, Confucianism. Dialogue with Eastern religions.

YEAR III COURSES

Compulsory Courses (34 Credits)

REL 311 The Pentateuch 3 Credits

Course Content

Literary, historical and theological study of the first five books of the Old Testament. Documentary hypothesis and alternate theories of authorship. The Pentateuchal traditions: Yahwist, Elohist, Priestly, Deuteronomic. The theology of the Pentateuch: God's covenant with Abraham, the Law at Sinai, and preparation for entry into Canaan.

Bibliography
  • Noth M., A History of Pentateuchal Traditions (Englewood Cliffs: Prentice Hall, 1981).
  • Von Rad G., The Problem of the Hexateuch and Other Essays (London: McGill-Queens University Press, 1966).
REL 312 The Literature & Theology of St. John 3 Credits

Course Content

Detailed study of the Fourth Gospel, the three Epistles of John, and the Apocalypse. The Johannine Theology: Incarnation, Life, Light, Truth, Love, the role of Jesus as the Word (Logos), dualism of light and darkness, eschatology and the life eternal. The historical background and the early Christian community behind the Johannine literature.

Bibliography
  • Brown R.E., The Gospel According to John (New York: Doubleday, 1966).
  • Carson D.A., The Gospel According to John (Grand Rapids: Wm. B. Eerdmans, 1991).
REL 313 Sacraments I: Baptism & Eucharist 3 Credits

Course Content

The nature and purpose of the sacraments. Baptism: the biblical foundation, grace and justification, ritual development and theology. The Eucharist: institution narrative and meaning, transubstantiation versus other interpretations, sacrifice and memorial, communion and unity in the Church, liturgical theology.

Bibliography
  • Rahner K., The Church and the Sacraments (New York: Herder and Herder, 1963).
  • Schmemann A., The World as Sacrament (London: Darton, Longman & Todd, 1966).
REL 314 Ethics of Human Life and Sexuality 3 Credits

Course Content

Ethical issues in biomedics: beginning of life (conception, contraception, abortion, artificial reproduction), end of life (euthanasia, suicide, capital punishment). Human sexuality: the Catholic teaching on sexuality, marriage and procreation. Homosexuality. Sexual abuse and its pastoral implications.

Bibliography
  • John Paul II, Encyclical Letter Evangelium Vitae (Rome, 1995).
  • Catholic Bishops' Conference, Pastoral Letter on Sexuality (Washington: USCCB, 1991).
REL 315 Family Morality and Marriage Theology 3 Credits

Course Content

The sacrament of matrimony: biblical foundations, consent and indissolubility, impediments to marriage, mixed marriages and ecumenical issues. The purposes of marriage: procreation and conjugal love. Family life: roles, duties, responsibilities. Divorce and remarriage. Pastoral ministry to families. Celibacy and religious life.

Bibliography
  • John Paul II, The Theology of the Body: Human Love in the Divine Plan (Boston: Pauline Books, 1997).
  • USCCB, Follow the Way of Love: A Pastoral Message to Families (Washington: USCCB, 1994).
REL 316 Trinity and Trinitarian Theology 3 Credits

Course Content

The doctrine of the Trinity: biblical foundations, development through the Councils (Nicaea, Constantinople, Chalcedon), Western and Eastern approaches. The persons of the Trinity: the Father as source, the Son's generation and incarnation, the Spirit's procession and sanctification. Trinitarian theology and Christian living.

Bibliography
  • Grillmeier A., Christ in Christian Tradition Vol. I (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 1989).
  • Torrance T.F., The Christian Doctrine of God (Edinburgh: T&T Clark, 1996).
REL 318 Modern, Recent, Contemporary Church History 3 Credits

Course Content

Church history from the Enlightenment to Vatican II and beyond. The challenges of modernism, industrialization, scientific advancement. The social teaching of the Church. Vatican Council II and its implementation. Church and state relations. Ecumenism and inter-religious dialogue. The role of the Church in contemporary society.

Bibliography
  • Chadwick O., A History of Christianity (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 1995).
  • Flannery A., ed. Vatican Council II: Conciliar and Post-Conciliar Documents (New York: Costello Publishing Company, 1988).
SEM 301 Canon Law: Church & Sacraments 3 Credits

Course Content

Canon law concerning the sacraments: validity and liceity, rites and ceremonies, minister and subject, effects of the sacraments. Canons on matrimony. Ordination and sacred orders. Clerical celibacy. Privileges and dispensations.

SEM 312 African Christian Theology 3 Credits

Course Content

The history of Christianity in Africa. African religious traditions and their relationship to Christianity. Inculturation of the Gospel in Africa. African theology and its major theologians. African Christology, ecclesiology, and pneumatology. Religious syncretism. The role of lay apostolate in Africa.

Bibliography
  • Kwesi Dickson, Theology in Africa (Maryknoll: Orbis Books, 1984).

SEM 315 Christology and Soteriology 3 Credits

Course Content

Advanced treatment of Christ: his nature and person, divinity and humanity, virgin birth, resurrection. Redemption theories: ransom, satisfaction (Anselm), substitution. Reconciliation and atonement. Christ as mediator and intercessor. The universal salvific will of God. Christ and world religions.

Bibliography
  • Boff Leonardo, Jesus Christ Liberator (Maryknoll: Orbis Books, 1978).

SEM 310 Pastoral Theology and Priestly Ministry 2 Credits

Course Content

The nature of pastoral theology. The priest as shepherd, servant, minister. Pastoral leadership and management. Ministry to the sick, suffering, and dying. Spiritual direction. The priest's personal sanctification. Celibacy and communal living.

GSS 006 Research Methodology and Academic Theses 3 Credits

Course Content

Research methods in theology and philosophy. Thesis development and structure. Primary and secondary sources. Literature review. Argumentation and critical analysis. Proper documentation and citation. Evaluation of research quality and validity.

GSS 007 Homiletics (Preaching) 2 Credits

Course Content

Principles of effective preaching. Sermon structure and preparation. Exegesis and homiletic application. Liturgical preaching. Preaching to diverse audiences. Use of examples, stories, and illustrations. Delivery and voice. Practice and feedback.

Elective Courses - Within Major Subject Area (2-3 Credits)

Course Content

Introduction to Biblical Hebrew alphabet, vowel systems, nouns, pronouns, verbs. Basic grammar and syntax. Reading and translation of selected Old Testament passages. Understanding Hebrew thought patterns.

Psychological foundations for pastoral work. Mental health and spiritual well-being. Counselling techniques and skills. Active listening and empathy. Crisis intervention. Addiction and recovery. Psychology of grief and mourning.

REL 319 Political & Cultural Systems in Africa 2 Credits

Course Content

African political structures and governance. Traditional leadership and governance systems. Colonial impact and post-colonial development. African cultural values and their implications for pastoral ministry. The role of the Church in promoting justice and peace.

Biblical foundations of healing ministry. Jesus as healer and the early Church's healing ministry. Sacraments of healing: Anointing of the Sick, Reconciliation. Spiritual and physical healing. The Eucharist as source and summit of Christian life. Biblical foundations and historical development. The theology of the Mass. The ministerial priesthood and the common priesthood of the faithful. Diaconal ministry and lay participation. The relationship between Eucharist and mission. Liturgical renewal and inculturation.

Bibliography
  • Power D.N., The Eucharistic Mystery: Revitalizing the Tradition (New York: Crossroad, 1992).

Theological foundations for interreligious dialogue. The uniqueness of Christ and universal salvation. Comparative study of Christianity with Islam, Judaism, Hinduism, Buddhism, and African Traditional Religion. The Church's approach to other religions. Mission and evangelization. Religious pluralism and relativism.

Bibliography
  • Kraemer H., The Christian Message in a Non-Christian World (Grand Rapids: Eerdmans, 1963).

  • Pope John Paul II, Redemptoris Missio (On the Permanent Validity of the Church's Missionary Mandate, 1990).
REL 418 Long Essay 6 Credits

Course Content

Independent research project on a theological topic chosen by the student with faculty supervision. Development of research question, literature review, methodology, and argumentation. Approximately 50 typed pages. Topics may include biblical studies, systematic theology, moral theology, church history, or pastoral theology. Defense of thesis before faculty committee.

Bibliography
  • Turabian K.L., A Manual for Writers of Research Papers, Theses, and Dissertations (Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 2013).
  • Barzun J. & Graff H.F., The Modern Researcher (Belmont: Wadsworth, 1992).
  • Booth W.C. et al., The Craft of Research (Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 2008).
SEM 316 The Letter to the Hebrews & Catholic Epistles 2 Credits

Course Content

The authorship, date, and purpose of Hebrews. Christology and priesthood theology. Faith and perseverance themes. Study of James, 1-2 Peter, 1-3 John, and Jude. Pastoral concerns and early Christian challenges. Eschatological hope and moral exhortation. Relationship to Pauline theology.

Bibliography
  • Attridge H.W., Hebrews: A Commentary on the Epistle to the Hebrews (Minneapolis: Fortress Press, 1989).
  • Johnson L.T., The Letter of James (New York: Doubleday, 1995).
  • Perkins P., First and Second Peter, James, and Jude (Louisville: Westminster John Knox Press, 1995).
SEM 401 Canon Law (Church & Marriage) [2 semesters] 4 Credits

Course Content

Advanced canon law studies. Marriage law: essential properties, consent, impediments, diriment and impedient. Matrimonial processes: declaration of nullity, dissolution. Clerical marriage and celibacy. Church governance and administration. Rights and obligations of clergy and laity. Penal law and sanctions.

Bibliography
  • Code of Canon Law (Latin-English Edition, 1983).
  • Coriden J.A. et al., The Code of Canon Law: A Text and Commentary (New York: Paulist Press, 1985).
  • Huels J.M., The Pastoral Companion: A Canon Law Handbook for Catholic Ministry (Quincy: Franciscan Press, 1995).
SEM 406 Missiology 3 Credits

Course Content

The theology of mission. Biblical foundations and historical development. The Church's missionary mandate. Evangelization and inculturation. Dialogue with other religions and cultures. Mission in Africa: challenges and opportunities. The role of lay people in mission. Contemporary mission strategies and methods.

Bibliography
  • Bosch D.J., Transforming Mission: Paradigm Shifts in Theology of Mission (Maryknoll: Orbis Books, 1991).
  • Shorter A., Toward a Theology of Inculturation (Maryknoll: Orbis Books, 1988).
  • Pope John Paul II, Redemptoris Missio (1990).
SEM 410 The Johannine Writings 2 Credits

Course Content

Advanced study of John's Gospel, Epistles, and Revelation. Johannine theology: light/darkness, life/death, love/hate. The Word made flesh. The Paraclete and pneumatology. Eschatology and realized eschatology. The Beloved Disciple and Johannine community. Literary and theological unity of the Johannine corpus.

Bibliography
  • Brown R.E., The Gospel According to John (New York: Doubleday, 1966).
  • Schnackenburg R., The Gospel According to St. John (New York: Crossroad, 1982).
  • Smith D.M., John (Nashville: Abingdon Press, 1999).
SEM 411 Seminar: Parish Administration 2 Credits

Course Content

Practical skills for parish administration. Financial management and budgeting. Personnel management and volunteer coordination. Facilities maintenance and development. Record keeping and sacramental registers. Pastoral planning and program development. Canon law requirements for parish administration.

Bibliography
  • Huels J.M., Parish Finance: Best Practices and Canon Law (Washington DC: CLSA, 2004).

  • USCCB, Co-Workers in the Vineyard of the Lord (2005).
SEM 412 Practical: Liturgy/RCIA 2 Credits

Course Content

Practical training in liturgical ministry. Rite of Christian Initiation for Adults (RCIA) process and implementation. Liturgical planning and preparation. Music and liturgical arts. Catechetical instruction for RCIA candidates. Mystagogy and post-baptismal formation. Special liturgical celebrations and seasons.

Bibliography
  • Rite of Christian Initiation for Adults (USCCB, 1988).
  • General Instruction of the Roman Missal (USCCB, 2011).
  • USCCB, Introduction to the Order of Mass (2015).

Pontificia Universitas Urbaniana - Examination Themes

The following 30 principal theological topics must be covered by professors and are referenced in written and oral examinations for the Bachelor's Degree in Sacred Theology.

Each institute affiliated with the Pontifical Urban University has the possibility to add three (3) additional local topics reflecting pastoral and cultural realities, subject to university approval.

1 Revelation and inspiration of Sacred Scripture in the light of Dei Verbum (cc. I-III); formation of the Canon; interpretation of the text.
2 Questions concerning the Pentateuch and new orientations of modern criticism; theology of Exodus. Historical books and their literary genre: composition and theology.
3 The function and message of the Prophets. The books of Isaiah, Jeremiah and Ezekiel: composition and theology.
4 The Wisdom Books and their literary genres; the Book of Psalms; Messianic and Christological "re-interpretations", liturgical and spiritual use.
5 The apostolic origin and historical value of the Gospels according to "Sancta Mater Ecclesia" (1964) and "Dei Verbum" (1965). Contents, articulation and theology of the three Synoptic Gospels and the Acts of the Apostles. Presentation and commentary on selected texts.
6 Content, articulation and theology of the five writings of St. John. Presentation and commentary on selected texts.
7 Content, articulation and theology of the Letters to the Romans, Galatians, I & II Corinthians, Colossians and Ephesians. Circumstances of the writing of I & II Thessalonians, Philippians, Philemon and Pastoral Letters. Presentation and commentary on selected texts.
8 Content and articulation of the Letter to the Hebrews and its theology of the Priesthood of Christ. Contents and circumstances of the writing of I & II Peter and James. Presentation and commentary on selected texts.
9 Explanation on the basis of the New Testament how Jesus, with his words and actions established the Kingdom of God, revealed the Father and Himself, and through His death, Resurrection, Ascension and the mission of the Holy Spirit, fulfilled His work.
10 Explain the images with which the Church of Christ is illustrated in Holy Scripture and in Tradition; show the nature and characteristic signs of the Church, taking also into consideration ecumenical perspectives.
11 The royal, prophetic and priestly office of all the faithful and the ministerial priesthood (Sacrament of Holy Orders). The relationship between the collegiality of the bishops and the primacy and infallibility of the Roman Pontiff.
12 The Church has received the mission to evangelise all peoples. Principles and orientations for dialogue with religions and cultures and for relationships with civil societies.
13 Point out the importance of the Fathers of the Church in the study of theology and describe briefly the doctrinal contributions of the most important Fathers.
14 Describe the outstanding events in the history of the Church and of the Missions.
15 The nature, function and fundamental principles of Canon Law in the Church. Make reference to the most important contents of the various books of the Code.
16 Explain briefly the truth of the One God: how it is possible for all human beings to know certainly this truth intuitively through the light of reason; how traces of one of its proofs can be found in the spiritual experience of different religions and peoples; and how finally this truth was arrived at from the complete revelation of the same living God in Salvation History.
17 Explain briefly the dogma of the Holy Trinity: how it has already been symbolized in the economy of the Old Testament; how it has been fully revealed in the New Testament; which principal Councils defined its essential elements; which could be the most common and acceptable theological explanations.
18 Explain the doctrine of the creation of all things by God, the nature and role of spiritual and material beings, of human beings and the doctrine of the constant Providence of God according to Revelation in Holy Scripture and in the Tradition of the Church.
19 Explain the dogma of Original Sin; in conformity with the data of Sacred Scripture, of the definitions of the Magisterium and of the most common and acceptable theological explanations; illustrate the doctrine of free will and human responsibility.
20 Explain the doctrine of the grace of God, with reference to the mystery of redemption and the gift of the Holy Spirit; add a summary sketch of the principal disputes concerning grace which have taken place in the course of Church history.
21 Explain the event of the Incarnation, Death and Resurrection of Jesus Christ Son of God. Show the principle elements of the Christological mystery in the dogmatic formulations of the relevant Church councils.
22 Explain the doctrine concerning the Virgin Mary as Mother of God and Mother of the Church, making reference to Council definitions and to the most recent documents of the Magisterium.
23 Explain in synthesis the traditional doctrine of the Church regarding the coming of Christ in glory and the fundamental points of Eschatology regarding both the single person as well as the entire creation.
24 Explain the nature and effects of the Sacraments in general, and in particular the Sacraments of Christian Initiation, and especially the Eucharist.
25 Principles which illustrate the theological meaning and pastoral value of the Liturgy as the action of Christ praising God and sanctifying persons; and how the action of the Church, through tangible signs, announces and brings into being the Mystery of Christ.
26 The moral qualification, which characterises every human act, finds its "origin" in the object, in the end and in the circumstances including the foreseeable consequences. Conditions of the imputability of the human act. Conscience and the principles for the judgement of conscience which make it the right norm for the action of the person. The formation of conscience.
27 Virtue and sin (nature, kind and dimensions) in Christian life. The conversion of the sinner and the Sacrament of Reconciliation.
28 Moral responsibility regarding human life and its integrity both as regards ones own life as well as the life of others. Particular reference to the moral problems connected to the beginning and the end of human life, with health care and medical interventions.
29 Human sexuality, the virtue of chastity, the Sacrament of Marriage. The solution of moral problems regarding sexuality in general and conjugal life in particular, keeping in mind also some indications of Canon Law.
30 Social Doctrine of the Church; the relationship between charity and justice, obligations to guarantee and favour peace.

General Observations

  • Dual Programme Operation: Since the Seminary operates both the University of Jos and the Pontificia Universitas Urbaniana programmes concomitantly, courses coded REL, GSS and SEM are applicable to both programmes.
  • Degree Selection: Students are requested to choose either of both degrees at the beginning of their studies.
  • Grade Conversion: Scores and grades are computed according to the degree chosen by the student. For homogeneity, the seminary employs the University of Jos scoring system in all examinations. Scores and grades sent to Rome are converted into the Roman grading scheme.
  • Transcript Requirements: To obtain the transcript of results and certificates, students must fulfil all academic requirements as enunciated above.
  • Academic Council Authority: The Academic Council reserves the right to withdraw any candidate from either of the degree programmes if his performance is not satisfactory.
